## Summary

The lifecycle state machine, the CP2-H1 anchor discipline, the tier-scale reuse for players, and the SnapshotBuffer port are genuinely solid — I traced each and list them at the end. Two things are wrong enough to gate on:

1. **The projectile detonation violates the FF matrix's one absolute rule** — "the caster is always excluded from their own ability" (Phase-4 spec amendment). `detonate` damages *every* alive player in the blast under FF, including the owner. A Fire player who fireballs a point-blank enemy in an FF-on room takes their own 22 damage — reproduced live this session (100 → 78 HP). Every non-projectile ability goes through `forFFTeammates`, which excludes the caster; the projectile path simply forgot.
2. **Player movement speed is coupled to the client's frame rate.** Input is consumed exactly once (`room.inputBuffer.clear()` every tick) and the client sends one input per Phaser `update()` (rAF). A 30 fps client — and mobile-landscape is a headline slice-1 target, spec §1 — fills at most 30 of 60 server ticks; the other 30 tick with `input === null`, i.e. zero movement. That player walks at literal half speed in a melee-positioning game.

No CRITICAL: nothing bricks the server, no remote crash, no wire overflow. I hunted the swap-remove/`i--` pattern through every new ability loop, the projectile loop and the burn-kill path; the input sanitizer against NaN/Infinity/garbage payloads; the flag-bit spaces; and the snapshot growth vs the egress budget — all sound. Ranked most-severe first.

## HIGH

- **H1 — FF-on projectiles damage their own caster; the spec's "never the caster" rule is broken on the projectile path.** `server/game/projectiles.js:60-66`: `detonate` loops `state.players`, skipping only `!p.alive` — there is no `ownerId` exclusion. Contrast `server/game/abilities.js:46-53` (`forFFTeammates`), which every instant ability uses and which skips the caster explicitly; the module header even documents "(never the caster)". The spec amendment (2026-07-19, "Friendly fire transmits damage + displacement, not statuses") states flat out: "The caster is always excluded from their own ability." **Failure scenario (reproduced by probe this session):** FF-on room, Fire player at (200,200), Orc at (220,200), aim right, cast Fireball → projectile detonates on the adjacent enemy ~1 tick later, caster is inside the 44 px `aoeRadiusPx` → caster HP 100 → 78, `pdmg` floating text on themselves. At point-blank melee range — exactly where Fireball is most tempting against a chasing enemy — Fire loses 22 HP per cast of *their own bread-and-butter special*, can down themselves into a horde, and the `damagePlayer` → down → bleed-out chain treats it like any enemy hit. The suite is green because `test/game/projectiles.test.js:76` positions `s.players[2]` in the blast but fires as `ownerId: 'p1'` — the one player the rule is about is never tested. **Fix:** in `detonate`, skip `p.id === pr.ownerId` (players still carry stable ids after soft-disconnect, so id-compare is correct even if the owner object was rebound). Add the regression test the FF suite is missing: owner inside own blast, FF on, HP unchanged.

- **H2 — input is consumed once per tick and cleared, so movement speed scales with the client's send rate; a 30 fps client moves at half speed.** `server/game/loop.js:70` (`room.inputBuffer.clear()` after every tick) + `server/game/players.js:167-186` (a tick with no buffered input means `dirX = dirY = 0` — the player does not move that tick) + `client/src/scenes/GameScene.js:224-243` (`_sendInput` runs once per Phaser `update()`, i.e. per rAF frame). **Failure scenario:** phone or throttled laptop rendering at 30 fps sends 30 inputs/s into a 60 Hz consume-and-clear loop → ≥30 of every 60 ticks see `null` input → held-W forward speed is ~50% of a 60 fps teammate's, and every WAN packet-coalescing burst (Socket.io over TCP) converts N frames of input into 1 moving tick + N−1 frozen ticks (latest-wins overwrites inside one window). Interpolation hides the jitter visually but the *distance* is gone: kiting, revive-run timing (`REVIVE_RANGE_PX` 48), and escape-from-down scenarios all silently degrade by device. This is inherited from ez-ctf's model (`C:\dev\ez-ctf\server\game\loop.js:66` clears the same way) and the amendment locked "ez-ctf's model" — but ez-ctf is a 4-player desktop shooter; Elementia declares mobile a first-class target and its melee/revive design is far more position-sensitive. **Fix (small):** persist the last-received input per player instead of clearing (clear only the edge-triggered action bits, or on disconnect — `rooms/index.js:235` already deletes on leave); or decouple the client send from rAF onto a fixed 60 Hz timer *and* accept the WAN-burst stutter. Persisting is the robust option and costs one Map you already have. Test: drive `tickGame` 60 ticks feeding input only every other tick; assert distance ≈ full speed, not half.

## MEDIUM

- **M1 — L3 boosts Fireball's damage but not its area/range; the amendment says "×1.3 damage AND area/range on the L1 specials".** `server/game/abilities.js:107-112` passes only `damage: Math.round(spec.damage * boost)` into `spawnProjectile`; `maxRangePx` and `aoeRadiusPx` come straight from `BALANCE.PROJECTILE.FIREBALL` (`server/game/projectiles.js:28-40`) untouched by `specialBoost`. Earth/Water/Wind get radius/range ×1.3 at L3 (`abilities.js:94, 118, 141`); Fire alone gets damage-only. **Failure scenario:** wave 6+, all four specials are supposed to feel the milestone; Fire's blast stays 44 px / 380 px while Water's cone grew 30% — a silent per-element imbalance the Phase-8 sweep will mis-attribute to base numbers. The L3 test (`test/game/abilities.test.js:226`) asserts damage and *slam radius* only, so it can't see this. **Fix:** scale `maxRangePx`/`aoeRadiusPx` by the boost at spawn (spawnProjectile already takes per-instance overrides for damage/burn — add the two radii), and extend the L3 test to cover Fire.

- **M2 — abilities are live in the build phase, so FF-on rooms allow pre-wave team-downing that the build-start restore cannot fix.** `server/game/tick.js:47-50` runs `tickPlayers` (and thus `trySpecial`/`trySecond`, `server/game/players.js:181-185`) in BUILD and WAVE_END; nothing in `abilities.js` checks phase; `restoreAllPlayers` runs only at build *start* (`server/game/phaseMachine.js:38`). The code comment calls this "harmless"; under FF it is not. **Failure scenario:** FF-on room, during build a Water player Tidal-Waves a teammate repeatedly (cooldown 9 s, damage 10 + shove) or Earth slams the group — a teammate downed at build-second-50 bleeds out 15 s, dies, and re-enters the fight on a 20 s+ respawn timer *mid-wave*, all before an enemy existed. The spec's phase model (§1: fight phase is when characters act) never intended combat consequences in build. High-trust design tolerates grief, but this also breaks the "build phase fully restores" invariant players will reason with. **Fix:** gate `trySpecial`/`trySecond`/`tryBasicAttack` (the damage-dealing actions — keep movement) on `state.phase === FIGHT`, or make FF teammate effects fight-only. One-line guard either way; add a test that a build-phase cast with FF on leaves teammates untouched.

- **M3 — a special cast in BUILD/WAVE_END spawns a projectile that never flies.** Same phase gap as M2, distinct symptom: `tickProjectiles` runs only inside `runFightSim` (`server/game/tick.js:32`), so a Fireball cast during build/waveEnd (client only *sends* action bits during fight, but the server accepts them any time — `server/index.js:154` does not check phase, and a modified client will) is appended to `state.projectiles`, rides every snapshot as a frozen `pr` entity, renders as a hanging fireball all build long, and is deleted by `initFight`'s `state.projectiles.length = 0` (`tick.js:23`). Cooldown consumed, ghost on the wire. **Fix:** falls out of M2's gate; otherwise tick projectiles in the same phases players tick.

- **M4 — the acceptance run certifies a different physics regime than production (20 Hz steps vs 60 Hz), and the knockback integrator is tick-rate-dependent.** `test/game/phase4Acceptance.test.js:102` drives `tickGame` with `dt = 50` ms. Movement (`speed × dt`) is rate-independent, but knockback/pull/dash displacement is `Σ v0·k^n·dt` with `k = KB_DECAY_PER_TICK = 0.85` applied *per tick* (`server/game/enemyMove.js:21,78`): total displacement is `v0·dt/(1−k)` — at dt=50 ms that is **3× the 60 Hz distance**, and Gale Dash's launch derivation hard-codes 60 Hz (`abilities.js:217`, `dashPx * 60 * 0.15`), so at dt=50 a dash travels ~3× `dashPx`. Today the scripted humans are EARTH+FIRE and never reach L4 (run ends at wave 6), so no KB ability actually fires in-acceptance — which is itself the gap: **the acceptance exercises neither Water nor Wind specials, no L4 second, and no L3-boosted combat** (it breaks the moment `wave >= 6`, the same tick L3 lands). The CP2-H3 lesson was "the acceptance must not hide its subsystem"; half the element roster and both level milestones' combat effects are hidden here. **Fix:** run the acceptance at `dt = 1000/60` (Phase-3 precedent of dt=100 predates KB-heavy content), extend one scripted run through wave 8+ with WATER/WIND humans, or add a focused integration test at 60 Hz asserting realized dash/knockback distance ≈ balance intent. Also convert KB decay to a time-based constant (`k^(dt/16.67)`) if variable-dt sims are meant to be valid at all.

## LOW

- **L1 — one-tick stale player flags on the wire.** `damagePlayer`/`die`/`revive` mutate `life` but `p.flags` is recomputed only in `tickPlayers` (`server/game/players.js:165,188`); a player downed by enemy melee (which runs *after* `tickPlayers`, `server/game/enemies.js:257-259`) is encoded that tick as `hp: 0, flags: 0`. On a gated emit the client shows a standing full-alpha dot at 0 HP for one snapshot. Set flags inside the transition functions (they already push fx there).

- **L2 — Gale Dash hard-codes the decay constant.** `server/game/abilities.js:217` uses `0.15` where the comment says `1 − KB_DECAY`; if `KB_DECAY_PER_TICK` (`enemyMove.js:21`) is ever retuned, dash distance silently detaches from `dashPx`. Import and compute `(1 - KB_DECAY_PER_TICK)`.

- **L3 — Ground Slam's FF check omits the target's body radius.** `abilities.js:101-103` tests teammates against `r`, enemies against `r + store.radius[i]` (`:96`). A teammate whose edge (but not center) is inside the ring is missed. Cosmetic inconsistency; align with the enemy test using `CONFIG.PLAYER_RADIUS`.

- **L4 — building on a player's tile teleports the body.** `placeStructure` validates tile occupancy against structures only (`server/game/structures.js:132`), never player positions; the newly solid tile ejects the resident player via the center-inside branch of `resolveTilePushout` (`collisionIndex.js:109-118`) with an anchor that is *also* inside the tile → deterministic pop to a face, a >32 px jump the client snap-teleports. A player fully walled in by teammates during build jitters inside the pocket. High-trust tolerable; reject placement on player-occupied tiles or accept and document.

- **L5 — fast closure can step a projectile past a knocked-back enemy.** Both bodies clamp to `MAX_STEP_PX = 31` px/tick, but the *relative* per-tick closure (fireball 7 px + enemy KB up to 31 px) can reach 38 px against a combined hit cross-section of ~24 px (`hitRadiusPx 12` + goblin radius) — `hitsEnemy` (`projectiles.js:72`) samples endpoints only. Rare (requires an enemy flung along the flight line); a segment-vs-circle test or halving the projectile step would close it.

- **L6 — `rescanCombos` stamps L2 retro-combos with `createdAt: 0`.** `phaseMachine.js:43` calls `rescanCombos(state)` with the default `now = 0` (`combos.js:52`), so retro-resolved Steam Vents/Grinders get epoch-zero `createdAt`/`attackReadyAt`. Harmless today (dormancy FIFO only orders marketplaces; ready-at-0 means ready), but pass the loop's `now` through `stepPhase`→`startBuildPhase` before anything sorts combat structures by age.

- **L7 — fx render at raw server coordinates while bodies render ~100 ms in the past.** `GameScene._onSnapshot` plays fx on arrival; interpolated enemies lag `INTERP_DELAY_MS` behind, so damage numbers lead the dot they belong to by a body-width at speed. Cosmetic; defer fx by the same delay if it reads badly in playtests.

## What I checked and found sound

The down→revive→death→respawn machine is watertight under adversarial tracing: lethal damage can only down (never skip to dead), damage to down/dead is ignored, the revive channel resets on abandonment and cannot tick from downed/dead teammates, death-vs-revive on the same tick resolves deterministically (expiry checked first), disconnect mid-down soft-converts to a bot without dangling state (`rooms/index.js:231-247`), and downed players are dropped as aggro targets the tick they fall (`enemies.js:193-199` filters `alive`), with `damagePlayer`'s `life !== 'up'` guard closing the same-tick race. The CP2-H1 anchor discipline is correctly replicated in `moveAndCollide` (pre-move anchor, then pushout, then arena clamp), player tick paths don't allocate (module-scoped scratch `Float64Array`s, memoised solid-fn), every swap-remove/`i--` revisit in abilities/projectiles/burn paths is correct, the socket input sanitizer rejects non-finite aim and coerces all bits so no NaN reaches cone math, `maxHttpBufferSize` is 10 KB, the leveling milestones land exactly on waves 1/3/6/8 with no downgrade and the L2 retro-rescan bumps `placedVersion`/the cost field via `destroyStructure`, PLAYER_FLAG bits collide with nothing (separate field from enemy flags), snapshot growth (`pr`, `lv`, fx `v`) is a rounding error against the 1.15-margin egress budget, and the SnapshotBuffer never extrapolates, rejects out-of-order ticks, and snap-teleports across respawn edges.

## Verdict

**CONDITIONAL GO.** H1 is a confirmed, probe-reproduced violation of the FF matrix's only absolute rule, hidden by a test that checks the wrong player — fix it and add the owner-in-blast regression before Phase 5 builds bots that will happily fireball their own feet. H2 is structural: decide *now* whether input persists or clears, because every Phase-5/6 system (bot input synthesis, economy actions) will layer onto this buffer, and retrofitting persistence later touches all of them. M2/M3 are one shared phase-gate; M1 is a two-line balance-fidelity fix; M4 is the CP2-H3 lesson recurring in milder form — the acceptance again looks away from part of what it certifies (Water/Wind kits, L3/L4 combat, production tick rate). The LOWs are cleanup. The core Phase-4 machinery — lifecycle, collision discipline, tier-scale reuse, wire format, interpolation — is the strongest work in the repo so far.
