# Handoff — Muddy Bog: the last fusion that isn't worth its ingredients

Date: 2026-08-30. Written at the end of a session that closed out the Grinder
with the project's first registered fusion verdict. Muddy Bog is now the only
fusion in the roster still measured NEGATIVE against the two structures it
consumes.

---

## READ THIS FIRST — the prior verdict on Bog is STALE

`docs/reviews/2026-08-28-fusion-roster-worth-retune.md` concluded:

> "**Muddy Bog — not fixed.** ... Damage saturates, root saturates, removing
> the lingering slow bought 0.014. **This needs a mechanic review, not another
> dial.**"

**That conclusion was measured while Bog's damage was gated behind its own
root.** The same review found the cause: `areaEntry.js` only pulsed damage at
an enemy this Bog had personally rooted, so total damage was
`root uptime x tick damage` with both factors saturating.

**That coupling was REMOVED on 2026-08-29 (commit `9ee93ad`).** Damage is now
gated on footprint PRESENCE only, independent of root, and `pulse.damage` went
12 → 28. So the "damage saturates / root saturates / resists both its levers"
finding describes a mechanic that no longer exists.

**Do not start from a mechanic redesign. Re-measure the dials first** — the
reason Bog resisted them may simply be gone. That is the cheapest possible
first step and it could close the whole question.

## Ideas, ranked — and what is already refuted

**Do not re-propose these five.** All were tested and refuted on 2026-08-28
(§2 of that review): "fusions lose a continuous damage field", "double the
throughput", "it's coverage, widen reach", "the CC effects are dead weight",
"the 2x2 footprint costs tower sites".

1. **Re-measure the damage lever now the coupling is gone.** Highest value,
   lowest cost, for the reason in READ THIS FIRST. If `pulse.damage` now
   responds where it previously flat-lined, Bog may need nothing else.

2. **Bog has ZERO damage margin.** Its root applies on footprint crossing and
   its damage on footprint presence — both the bare 2x2, no margin at all,
   where Firepit has `marginPx: 15` and Steam Vent `cloudMarginPx: 15`. Any
   knockback (Water Geyser, Vortex release, Grinder eject, several player
   abilities) shoves a still-rooted enemy off the footprint, where it takes
   nothing. **This is exactly the Grinder's edge-root defect in mirror image**
   — that structure held enemies at 160px while only damaging at 55px, and
   fixing the mismatch is what made it work.

3. **Apply the dwell-time principle from the Grinder.** The generalisable
   finding of 2026-08-29/30: throughput increases only pay off where a HOLD
   exists, and the hold area must match the damage area. Critically, this does
   NOT contradict refuted idea #3 above — that one widened Grinder's *intake*
   (pull) radius and made things worse. Widening a *damage* radius over an
   already-held population is the opposite lever and is what worked. Bog has a
   hold (its root) and a damage field, so it is structurally the same shape as
   the Grinder was.

4. **Ask why maze B specifically.** Bog is the roster's only fusion worse on
   B, with higher occupancy there. Before tuning, check the absolute scores
   against the control's own maze swing — on the Grinder the "maze split"
   turned out to be entirely the CONTROL's degradation, and the rejected
   edge-root config had a swing of exactly zero. Costs nothing but reading.

5. **Feel, not numbers.** `pulse.damage` is now 28 against a goblin's 12 HP,
   so basic enemies die to the Bog's first pulse. That is a large change in
   feel that no metric here can judge, and it has never been playtested.

## Method — non-negotiable, learned the hard way

Ad-hoc probes at n=144 **cannot resolve a maze-A effect in this project**.
The detection floor at that n is ~0.203 (maze A) and ~0.329 (maze B), and
this session burned most of a day producing "positive but unresolvable"
numbers before noticing. A properly powered family is n=3200/arm/maze and
costs **~2 minutes per 12,800 runs**. Go registered from the start.

Full mechanics, including three traps that silently void a verdict, are in
the `elementia-registered-sweep-mechanics` memory. The short version:

- Write the prereg, validate it against `prereg/_schema.json`, **commit it,
  THEN run.** A late or invalid prereg degrades quietly to a descriptives
  dump; it does not error.
- `dirty` counts UNTRACKED files. Philip's uncommitted `art/` assets alone
  are enough to make `analyze.mjs` refuse. Stash with `-u`, run, pop, and
  verify the restore.
- The first sweep's own store output dirties the tree for a second sweep run
  straight after. Park it outside the repo in between.

Templates to copy: `test/harness/prereg/grinder-r3-worth.json` and
`test/harness/bench/specs/grinder-r3-worth.json`.

## Recommended setup

- **Model: Opus 5.** The first step is a judgement call (is the stale
  saturation finding actually stale?) followed by prereg design, where the
  failure mode is silent. This session retracted two findings and mis-sized
  every probe; that is the class of error that costs whole sessions.
- **Subagents: one, optional.** The thread is sequential (re-measure → decide
  → register → run), so no parallel slicing. A single read-only `Explore`
  (Sonnet) to map `areaEntry.js` plus Bog's balance entries and report back
  would keep a lot of file-reading out of main context.
- **Review: yes, adversarial, Opus 5 or Fable 5**, but only on a CLAIMED
  VERDICT, not on sweep mechanics. This project's pattern — demonstrated
  twice in one day this session — is that the first draft of a finding is
  wrong in a way a second pass catches.
